      Model Lab

      Everything on this page, from training and cross-validation to explanations, runs in your browser in plain JavaScript (ml.js, no ML libraries). Your keystrokes never need to leave your device.

      The training data is a simulated study. Its assumptions come from published sleep research and are listed below. The pipeline is built so real check-ins can replace it with no code changes.

      1 · The data: KeyPulse Hackathon Study (simulated)

      Participants–
      Labelled check-ins–
      Hours awake (max)–
      Dual-task check-ins–

      40 people check in every ~2.5 h through a 34-hour hackathon. ~30% of check-ins are randomly assigned a dual-task (type while counting backwards by 7s). Labels: KSS sleepiness (1–9) and NASA-TLX mental demand (0–100).

      Simulation assumptions
      • Two-process model of sleep regulation (Borbély): sleep pressure builds while awake (τ = 18.2 h) and clears during sleep (τ = 4.2 h), combined with a 24 h circadian rhythm (peak ~18:00, low point ~06:00). That's why you see the 4 am peak and a "second wind" the next afternoon.
      • People differ in how badly sleep loss hits them (Van Dongen et al., 2004): each person gets a stable vulnerability factor.
      • Each person has their own typing style (speed, hold time, rhythm), so raw numbers aren't comparable across people.
      • Reaction lapses and keypress steadiness respond most to fatigue. Pauses, rhythm and speed respond most to workload. Caffeine temporarily masks sleepiness. 40% of participants nap.
      • Label noise: KSS ±0.7 points, TLX ±8 points (self-reports are noisy).

      Model card

      • Intended use: personal, self-referenced wellness feedback ("am I off my normal, and why?"). Not a medical device or diagnosis.
      • Training data: simulated (see assumptions). Real-world accuracy will be lower. Next step: collect labelled check-ins from real users and retrain with the same pipeline.
      • Needs: 2 calibration check-ins per person. Physical keyboards only (phone keyboards hide key timings).
      • Known confounds: unfamiliar keyboards, injuries, alcohol/medication, typing in a second language.
      • Privacy: only timings and key categories are used. Inference runs on-device.
